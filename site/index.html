A tablet that runs only learning apps.

Duckling Tablet turns an Android tablet, an iPad or a Windows device into a locked-down learning device for one child. It runs a library of learning apps made by one developer for his own daughter, with no ads and nothing to buy inside.

Parents manage every device from a web portal, choose which websites are allowed, and can ask for an app that does not exist yet.

What the child sees

The home screen shows the apps that are installed, the child's name, the colors the child picked, and any websites a parent has allowed. There is nothing else on it: no browser, no store accounts, no notifications from other apps.

The Store inside the tablet lists every Duckling app. The child installs and removes apps freely, because every app in the library is made and checked by the same person. A parent can hide a particular app from a particular device.

Every app works offline once installed. A friendly narrator voice reads instructions and questions aloud; lines that include the child's name are spoken on the spot and never stored.

Apps keep their own progress on the device. Most climb a ladder of levels: four right answers in a row move up, two misses move down, so the work stays at the edge of what the child can do.

The app library

Twenty-three apps at launch, covering reading, writing, math, science, thinking games, music, voice, drawing and the camera. New apps join the Store for every family as they are finished.

What the parent does

You sign in to the parent portal with an email link; there is no password to remember. From there you set up the child's profile (first name, grade, starting levels and a theme), pair each tablet with a six-digit code, and see every device at a glance.

  • Allow specific websites, which appear as tiles on the home screen
  • Turn on travel mode for a few hours, which lifts the website list
  • Set daily time limits and a bedtime
  • Hide an app from one device, or install one remotely
  • Release a device, which removes the lockdown on its next check-in

On the tablet itself, a parent PIN guards the lockdown, the website list, the child's profile, the subscription and removal. The child never needs the PIN for ordinary use.

If your child needs an app that is not in the library, describe it in the portal. Each request becomes a ticket for the developer, you can follow its status, and when the app ships it can install itself on your devices. One request is open per family at a time, so the queue stays fair.

How to get it

Duckling Tablet is being prepared for Google Play, the App Store and Windows. Store links will appear here as each one goes live. A device you already own is enough; the app does the locking down.

  1. Install the Duckling Tablet app on the child's device and start the free trial.
  2. Pair it with the six-digit code from the parent portal. The starter apps install on their own.
  3. Follow the setup steps on screen to lock the device to Duckling. The steps differ by platform and each one is checked off as it is done.

  • Android tablets

    Android 10 or newer. Duckling becomes the home screen and pins itself in place. Removing it releases the tablet the same way.

  • iPad

    Uses Apple's Screen Time controls through the Duckling app, with optional Guided Access. No supervision profile and no device wipe.

  • Windows

    Windows 10 or 11. A signed installer sets up a child account and a kiosk that opens only Duckling. Billing on Windows goes through a card payment on the web.

Pricing

$5per month, per family

One subscription covers every device in the family on every platform. The first 7 days are free. You can cancel at any time in the store you subscribed through, or in the parent portal; a device is always released before it loses access, so a child is never left locked in a dead app.

Billing runs through Google Play on Android, the App Store on iPad, and a card payment on the web for Windows.

Privacy, in short

  • No ads, no trackers, no third-party analytics in the apps or the portal.
  • About the child we keep a first name, a grade, starting levels and a theme. Nothing else is asked for.
  • Microphone and camera data stay on the device. Camera Fun pictures live in the tablet's own gallery.
  • Narrator lines that contain the child's name are never stored on our servers.
  • Deleting the family account deletes everything, and releases every device first.

The full privacy policy lists every piece of data, who processes it and for how long. The children's privacy notice is the short version written for the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act.

How to set up an Android tablet with Duckling

The steps that lock an Android tablet to Duckling, as the parent area shows them. Pairing, the parent PIN, pinning, a screen lock, and the home screen, then what the child sees and how to undo it.

Install Duckling Tablet from Google Play on the child's tablet. It needs Android 10 or newer. Setup has three parts, all on the tablet. The first pairs the tablet with the family account, the second sets the parent PIN, and the third locks the tablet so the child stays in Duckling. Each lockdown step gets a check mark when it is done.

Pair the tablet with the family

Open Duckling and type a parent's email. A six-digit code goes to that email and works for 10 minutes. Type the code, then the child's first name and age. The narrator uses the name. The age sets where every app starts, two years below the child's age, so a 7-year-old starts at kindergarten level and moves up with each right answer. A new family starts a 7-day free trial with no card.

A second tablet joins the family with a code from the parent portal instead. In the portal at parents.ducklingtablet.com, under Devices, press Make a pairing code. The code works once and expires in 15 minutes. On the tablet, choose I have a code from the website and type it.

Choose the parent PIN

The tablet then asks for a four-digit parent PIN, typed twice. The PIN guards the parent area on every tablet in the family. The child never needs it for ordinary use. The PIN can be changed in the parent portal, and a forgotten PIN is reset there under Settings. The tablet picks up the new PIN at its next check-in.

The three lockdown steps

With the PIN set, the parent area opens on its Lockdown card. Until the three steps are done, a child can leave Duckling with the Home button. Each step is checked when you come back to this screen. With all three done, the child cannot leave Duckling.

1. Keep Duckling pinned

Press Turn on. Android asks once to confirm pinning. After that, Duckling re-pins itself on every start. Android's pinning keeps one app's screen in view until it is unpinned. Google's Android Help page on pinning and unpinning screens says that when app pinning is on, the tablet asks for its PIN, pattern, or password before an app can be unpinned.

2. A screen lock the child does not know

Press Open settings. Android's security settings open. Set a PIN, pattern, or password that the child does not know. Unpinning asks for it, and that is what keeps the child inside Duckling. In the same settings, turn on App pinning. The Android Help page lists the switch under Security, then Advanced, then App pinning, and says to ask the device's manufacturer when it is not there.

3. Make Duckling the home screen

Press Continue. Android shows its list of home apps. Choose Duckling Tablet. The Home button and the recent-apps screen then lead back to Duckling. This step restarts Duckling, and the parent area comes back on its own.

What the child sees

The home screen shows the installed apps, the child's name, the colors the child picked, and any websites a parent has allowed. There is no browser, no store account, and no notification from another app. The child installs and removes apps from the Store inside the tablet, which lists every Duckling app. A parent can hide a particular app from a particular tablet.

Other apps, with daily minutes

Apps installed on the tablet before Duckling can appear on the home screen with a daily allowance. The choice is made in the parent portal under that tablet. While another app is open the lockdown is lifted, and Duckling comes back on its own when the time ends. For that it needs Android's permission to appear over other apps. The Other apps card in the parent area has an Open settings button that leads to it.

Microphone, camera, and Wi-Fi

Apps that listen or take pictures need the microphone and the camera. The Microphone and camera card in the parent area shows whether each one is on. Its Continue button brings up Android's request for each one. Sound and pictures stay on the tablet.

A Wi-Fi network with its own sign-in page is joined from the Wi-Fi card. Press Choose a Wi-Fi network, pick the network, tap Sign in to network in the notification that appears, and finish its page. Then press Back until Duckling is showing. The lockdown comes back on its own.

Managing the tablet from a phone

Apps, websites, the daily minutes for other apps, the child's level, and a second parent are chosen in the parent portal. The parent area shows a QR code that opens it, and the portal offers to add itself to the phone's home screen. Changes reach the tablet within seconds while it is on and online. A tablet that gets no push notifications picks them up when it is opened and every three hours. The Sync with the website button fetches them now.

Billing on Android goes through Google Play. The subscription is $5 a month for every tablet in the family, after the 7 free days. The Subscribe button is in the parent area, and the subscription renews each month until it is cancelled in Google Play under Payments and subscriptions.

Removing Duckling

Remove Duckling from this device, at the bottom of the parent area, lifts the lockdown, tells the website, and clears the apps and data. Android then asks whether to uninstall Duckling. The family account keeps its settings, and the tablet can be paired again later.

Removing the tablet in the parent portal, under Devices, leaves the tablet as it is. Changes from the portal stop reaching it and its narrator voice turns off, while its apps, PIN, and lockdown stay. Its parent area then asks for a new pairing code, so it can join a family again.
